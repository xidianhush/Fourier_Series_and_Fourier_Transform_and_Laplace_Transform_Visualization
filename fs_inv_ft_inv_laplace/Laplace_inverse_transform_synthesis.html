Signal synthesis by the inverse Laplace transform: a linearly weighted integral of the complex exponentials e^{st} spread along the vertical line \mathrm{Re}\,s=\sigma in the s-plane, weight = X(s)\,ds/2\pi j

f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi j}\int_{\sigma-j\infty}^{\sigma+j\infty}X(s)e^{st}\,ds=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}X(\sigma+j\omega)e^{\sigma t}e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega=\lim_{\Delta\omega\to 0}\sum_{n}X(\sigma+jn\Delta\omega)\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi}e^{(\sigma+jn\Delta\omega)t}
— weight = \frac{X(s)ds}{2\pi j} (infinitesimal); e^{\sigma t} makes the whole vector chain breathe; chain endpoint = f(t)

Signal, abscissa of convergence \sigma & frequency granularity (hero sliders: \Delta\omega and \sigma)

The observation window \pm T_w = k × this preset's "tail time" (the time the signal needs to decay to negligible) — it is the "Window ±Tw" row in the readouts. This quantity is invented for this page: the Riemann sum is taken to be \approx f(t) only inside the window, so the error readout, the time-domain vertical scaling, the horizontal half-width X_w=\mathrm{clamp}(T_r+T_w,\ 3T_w,\ 6T_w), and the playback wrap range are all decided by it. Push k beyond the valid region and the error readout blows up while the faded out-of-window segment on the time-domain plot grows longer — which is exactly the manual experiment for judging "is \Delta\omega small enough".

The inverse transform writes f(t) as a "continuous sum" of the complex exponentials e^{st} along the vertical line \mathrm{Re}\,s=\sigma in the s-plane: weight = \frac{X(s)ds}{2\pi j}. σ must lie inside the region of convergence ROC (the green band in the s-plane): for causal signals \sigma > \sigma_{\max}; two-sided signals sit between two poles. Each basis vector v_n rotates (e^{j\omega t}) while the whole chain breathes (e^{\sigma t}); \sigma=0 degenerates to the inverse Fourier transform. The computation discretizes with \Delta\omega, giving 2N+1 components. Drag \Delta\omega down: the spectral lines grow denser, the weights shrink in proportion to \Delta\omega, the chain segments shrink below visibility (use the magnifier), and the Riemann sum hugs the integral; the price is a strict period T_r=2\pi/\Delta\omega for the sum, with replica weights e^{-\sigma kT_r}: \sigma>0 suppresses future replicas but amplifies past ones by e^{+\sigma T_r}.

Custom f(t) (the input box appears only when this preset is selected): the syntax is a mini math expression, not LaTeX — variables t a w0; constants pi π e tau; functions exp sqrt abs sin cos tan sinh cosh tanh log log10 heaviside (or u) sign min max pow hypot; operators + − * / ^ (** also accepted), unary ±, implicit multiplication like 2t, 3sin(t), 2(t+1). LaTeX-flavored input is straightened out (\sin, e^{-2t}, { } [ ] all accepted). X(\sigma+j\omega) has no closed form, so this page computes it on the fly by midpoint quadrature over ±60 s (h\,\omega_{\max}\le 0.5); as a result, the ROC is only a numerical estimate and poles/zeros are not drawn; when σ hugs the ROC boundary, the signal is too long, or non-finite values occur, hints appear in the readouts and below the input box. To judge whether \Omega_{\max}/\Delta\omega suffices, still watch the "in-window \max|\mathrm{Re}(s-f)|". The definition used is the two-sided transform \int f(t)e^{-st}\,dt: multiply by u(t) yourself for causal signals.

Playback (t drives the complex-plane vector chain)

During playback t wraps inside the observation window \pm T_w (one lap in 8 s at 1×); the t slider can locate anywhere on the full axis \pm X_w; you can also drag directly on the time-domain panel below to scrub. e^{\sigma t} varies with t: for \sigma>0 the whole chain inflates over time, for \sigma<0 it shrinks, and at \sigma=0 it only rotates.

Display

Frozen scale: the complex-plane scale locks onto the largest extent the chain reaches within the observation window \pm T_w, so while dragging/playing t the chain visibly breathes with e^{\sigma t} and endpoint sizes can be compared across t (a scale bar at the left gives absolute values). The price: when t walks to the far end of the window and e^{\sigma t} has squeezed the chain small, the chain shrinks to a dot or even vanishes (out-of-window parts beyond the scale are clipped) — that is not a drawing bug, it really is that small; use the magnifier to inspect the segments then. Uncheck to return to "per-frame auto-scaling": the chain always fills the panel, but sizes are not comparable.

How to read the three panels

Top left: complex plane. v_n=X(\sigma+jn\Delta\omega)\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi}e^{(\sigma+jn\Delta\omega)t} (n=-N\ldots N) join tip-to-tail into a chain whose endpoint = the synthesized s(t); the red vector = s(t), and its projection onto the Re axis is the height of the time-domain curve. The scale is frozen by default at the largest chain magnitude inside the observation window (the scale bar at bottom left gives absolute values), so both the chain's breathing and the endpoint size are comparable across t; chain parts beyond the scale are clipped. When the endpoint s(t) would draw shorter than 3 px, it becomes a dashed schematic arrow (true direction, fixed 12 px) labeled "\approx 0 (magnified N×)"; only when it is down to a white dot is it truly undrawable. All v_n share the breathing factor e^{\sigma t}: during playback the whole chain scales as it rotates. When \Delta\omega is small the segments shrink below visibility — the circular magnifier at top right redraws the chain near the mouse at ×g magnification.
Top right: 3D s-plane. The horizontal plane is the s-plane: the \sigma=\mathrm{Re}\,s axis and the \omega=\mathrm{Im}\,s axis (each axis scaled independently, framing consistent with the top-left panel); only bars with |\omega|\le the ω half-width are drawn (the weights of the remaining components already lie in the tails). On the floor: green band = ROC, × = poles, ○ = zeros, cyan line = the integration path \mathrm{Re}\,s=\sigma (adjustable by slider; leaving the ROC means no convergence); with "custom f(t)" selected the ROC is a numerical estimate and poles/zeros are not drawn. The upright bar heights = weights |X(\sigma+jn\Delta\omega)|\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi} (they shrink in proportion to \Delta\omega = infinitesimal), colors = phases \arg X(\sigma+jn\Delta\omega) (same scheme as the Fourier page's 3D fence; for real signals the phase is odd-symmetric in ω, so the two sides mirror each other), and the gray curtain = |X|\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi} over continuous ω; switch to "raw |X|" to see that the curtain itself does not change with \Delta\omega. Drag inside the panel to rotate the view (Shift+arrow keys also work); hover a bar → the corresponding chain segment is highlighted in white; click a bar → mute that component; double-click the panel to restore all. Bars and the complex-plane vector chain share the same colors (both bucketed into 24 bins by the phase arg X), so a hovered bar matches its chain segment in color as well.
