Signal synthesis by the inverse Fourier transform: a linearly weighted integral of the complex exponentials e^{j\omega t} spread over the whole \omega axis, weight = F(j\omega)\,d\omega/2\pi

f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}F(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega=\lim_{\Delta\omega\to 0}\sum_{n}F(jn\Delta\omega)\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi}e^{jn\Delta\omega t}
— weight of each component = \frac{F(jn\Delta\omega)\,\Delta\omega}{2\pi} (infinitesimal); vector-chain endpoint = f(t)

Signal & frequency granularity (hero slider: \Delta\omega)

The inverse transform writes f(t) as a "continuous sum" of the complex exponentials e^{j\omega t} at every frequency: the weight of each frequency = \frac{F(j\omega)\,d\omega}{2\pi}. The computation discretizes with \Delta\omega, giving 2N+1 components (N=\lfloor\Omega_{\max}/\Delta\omega\rfloor). Drag \Delta\omega down: the spectral lines grow denser, the weights shrink in proportion to \Delta\omega, the chain segments in the complex plane shrink below visibility (use the magnifier), and the Riemann sum hugs the integral; the price is that the sum becomes strictly periodic with T_r=2\pi/\Delta\omega (yellow bracket), and when T_r is too small the periodic replicas \sum_k f(t-kT_r) walk into the observation window (red bracket).

Playback (t drives the complex-plane vector chain)

During playback t wraps inside the observation window \pm T_w (one lap in 8 s at 1×); the t slider can locate anywhere on the full axis \pm X_w; you can also drag directly on the time-domain panel below to scrub. The Riemann sum is strictly periodic with T_r=2\pi/\Delta\omega; what lies outside the window consists of periodic replicas (weight 1 — this page has \sigma=0, without the e^{\pm\sigma kT_r} amplification of the Laplace page).

Display

A scale bar (|s| = \ldots) always sits at the bottom left of the complex plane, so absolute values stay readable however the scale changes. "Frozen scale" is on by default (consistent with the inverse Laplace page): the scale locks onto the largest extent the chain reaches within the observation window \pm T_w, so endpoint sizes can be compared across t and both pages draw the same size under the same parameters. The price is that once t leaves 0 the chain shrinks a lot — in pure Fourier synthesis the chain magnitude fluctuates through phase cancellation (34%–99% within the window, measured); that is not a drawing bug, it really is that small. Uncheck to return to per-frame auto-scaling (the chain always fills the panel, but sizes are not comparable). When the endpoint s(t) would draw shorter than 3 px, a dashed schematic arrow labeled "\approx 0" is drawn instead.

How to read the three panels

Top left: complex plane. v_n=F(jn\Delta\omega)\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi}e^{jn\Delta\omega t} (n=-N\ldots N) join tip-to-tail into a chain whose endpoint = the synthesized s(t); chain-segment color = that component's initial phase at t=0, \arg F(jn\Delta\omega) (the spectral lines and the 3D fence share the same colors); the red vector = s(t), and its projection onto the Re axis is the height of the time-domain curve. The scale bar at bottom left gives absolute values. When \Delta\omega is small the segments shrink below visibility — the circular magnifier at top right redraws the chain near the mouse at ×g magnification.
Top right: spectrum. Spectral lines = weights |F(jn\Delta\omega)|\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi} (they shrink in proportion to \Delta\omega = infinitesimal); the gray envelope = |F(j\omega)|\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi}; switch to "raw |F(j\omega)|" to see that the envelope itself does not change with \Delta\omega. Hover a line → the same-colored chain segment is highlighted in white; click a line → mute that component. With "3D spectrum fence" checked, the top-right panel switches to a 3D view: a same-colored fence standing in the \sigma=0 plane (stem height = weight) plus a gray envelope curtain; the horizontal plane spans the j\omega axis and the \sigma axis — drag inside the panel to rotate the view (yaw/pitch). The σ axis is the slot reserved for the inverse Laplace transform, and the inverse Laplace page already puts that stage to use: there the top right is a true 3D s-plane with the whole fence standing on the integration path \mathrm{Re}\,s=\sigma, bar height = |X(\sigma+jn\Delta\omega)|\frac{\Delta\omega}{2\pi}, color = phase \arg X; dragging the σ slider slides the whole fence along σ (on this page the 3D fence stays fixed at \sigma=0, colored by the t=0 initial phases). Click-to-mute works only in 2D mode; double-click to restore all muted components works in both.
Bottom: time domain. Colored = the Riemann-sum synthesis \mathrm{Re}\,s(t) (strictly periodic with T_r=2\pi/\Delta\omega, i.e. the Poisson sum \sum_k f(t-kT_r)); gray dashed = the ideal f(t). Red bracket = observation window \pm T_w; yellow bracket = one Riemann period T_r; green dashed = the t=0 origin, doubling as the magnitude axis (ticks labeled on its left); vertical axis = \mathrm{Re}\,s(t) (scaled over the whole axis; measured values outside the window are no larger than inside, so the scaling is not corrupted). Drag to scrub t.
